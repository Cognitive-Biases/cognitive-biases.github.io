# Editorial policy

This project is a public reference, not a content farm. Every new page or substantial rewrite should be useful on its own and traceable to evidence.

## How we write

Write in plain, natural English. Use a calm, semi-formal tone. Prefer a concrete example to abstract wording.

A good entry should separate:

- what the concept means;
- what the reviewed evidence supports;
- where the finding may not apply;
- a practical question or check a reader can use;
- the sources we relied on.

Avoid claims such as "your brain always", "this bias proves", "people with this bias" or "this technique will remove the bias" unless a source genuinely supports that level of certainty.

## Originality rule

Do not copy source prose into the library.

Research is used to learn the facts, limits and terminology. The final explanation must then be written from those notes in our own words.

Short quotations are allowed only when the exact wording is important. They must be clearly marked, attributed and kept brief.

Do not lightly rewrite a sentence by changing a few words. If a source sentence is still visible in the structure of the final text, rewrite from the underlying idea again.

## Source handling

Prefer primary research, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, respected academic reference works and official datasets.

Secondary articles can help discover a topic, but they should not be the only support for a scientific claim when stronger sources are available.

For each reviewed claim, record enough source information to find it again. Keep the publication year, title and stable link or DOI when available.

## What we say when evidence is uncertain

Uncertainty is part of the content, not something to hide.

Useful wording includes:

- "The studies we reviewed suggest..."
- "This effect is well established in..."
- "The evidence is mixed."
- "The finding appears to depend on..."
- "We found good evidence for the effect, but not for every proposed explanation."
- "We have not reviewed this entry yet."

Do not turn an editorial evidence label into a fake scientific score.

## Legacy content

The existing library contains older generated educational descriptions. They remain useful as a starting corpus, but "generated" does not mean "reviewed".

When an entry receives an evidence review, its visible summary should also be checked and rewritten where needed so the top of the page does not contradict the reviewed material.

Legacy content should be improved in batches. Do not claim that the whole corpus has been independently verified until it has.

## Duplicate and copied content checks

Before publishing new editorial material:

1. Check that the text is not an exact duplicate of another entry in this repository.
2. Search a distinctive sentence from the draft on the public web when practical.
3. If the same wording appears on an older site owned by this project, either redirect or canonicalise the old page, or rewrite the new page so the sites do not compete with each other.
4. If wording appears on an unrelated source, rewrite it from the evidence notes before publication.

Automatic checks can catch exact duplicates inside the repository. They cannot prove that prose is original on the wider web, so editorial review still matters.

## Licensing

The current project content is published under the licence in the repository. Attribution and reuse rules must be preserved on public data and documentation pages.

A licence does not give us permission to copy third-party text. Sources support facts and claims; they are not a pool of prose to reuse.

If the project later separates an open data layer from a more protected editorial layer, that change must be explicit in the repository and on the public Data page before any release uses the new terms.

## Final review checklist

Before a reviewed entry or research note is published, confirm that:

- the main claim matches the cited evidence;
- limitations are visible where they matter;
- the prose is our own;
- practical advice is framed as a check or procedure, not a guaranteed cure;
- related concepts are not being treated as identical;
- the review date is present;
- links still resolve;
- the language is understandable without specialist knowledge.
