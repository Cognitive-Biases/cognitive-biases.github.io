# Research editor

Use this skill when reviewing a new paper, dataset, benchmark or research report for possible inclusion in Cognitive Biases.

## Goal

Turn a source into a careful editorial proposal. Do not turn every source into content.

## Process

1. Read the primary source when available.
2. Identify what was actually tested and on whom or what.
3. Note the main result and the important limits.
4. Find the closest existing concept, comparison or decision context in the repository.
5. Decide whether the source confirms, narrows, challenges or extends what we currently say.
6. Draft new wording from the research notes rather than from source sentences.
7. Record the source and review date.
8. Run the repository checks before proposing publication.

## Output

Return a compact review with:

- source;
- related library entries;
- what we learned;
- what remains uncertain;
- proposed repository changes;
- publication recommendation: accept, hold or reject.

## Rules

Write for people. Use ordinary language on public pages.

Do not invent evidence strength. Do not copy source prose. Do not call a result causal unless the study supports that claim. Do not generalise one AI model or one experiment to all people or systems.

When evidence is mixed, say that it is mixed. When nothing should change, recommend no change.
